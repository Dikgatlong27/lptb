# LPTB Solutions Value Assessment

Prepared 10 September 2026

## Current conclusion

**A defensible rand valuation of LPTB Solutions cannot be established from the records reviewed.** The evidence supports specific project prices, invoices and proposed commercial arrangements. It does not provide reconciled revenue, sustainable profit, a current balance sheet or sufficient information to value the company's ownership interests.

The strongest documented project figure is **R250,000 for the completed initial YaBatho development scope**. This is a project consideration recorded in commercial documents, not LPTB's company valuation.

## Important distinctions

The R100,000 contributions and R150,000 outstanding amount are components of the R250,000 consideration. Adding all three would count the same project twice.

The terms describe a 10% YaBatho venture interest following the transaction. This cannot be valued as 10% of the software's R250,000 consideration: the price of a software deliverable does not establish the equity value of its operating venture. The readable agreement contains blank signature fields, while the later handover schedule refers to signed terms. The executed agreement, completion of transaction conditions and current ownership record therefore remain to be checked.

Neither a tender price nor a quotation establishes a won contract, revenue or company value. Customer-owned code and assets also cannot automatically be treated as LPTB-owned intellectual property.

## LPTB's commercial strengths

LPTB has a documented progression from websites, branding and small-business support to connected web and mobile platform work. Its founder has direct experience gathering requirements, coordinating developers, building interfaces and supporting deployments.

The YaBatho handover provides evidence of a substantial multi-application delivery effort. The Chairman Group retainer provides evidence of continuing support work. LVSIFT demonstrates phased development from a public website into user-account capabilities and a proposed learning-system integration. These are useful commercial credentials, although no independent customer outcome metrics or overall profitability figures were available.

## Information needed for a valuation

1. Financial statements or reconciled accounts for the latest two to three years and the current year to date.
2. Revenue received and invoiced, separated into development fees, retainers, hosting or other cost recoveries and once-off services.
3. Operating costs, subcontractor payments, owner compensation, profit and cash flow.
4. Cash, equipment, receivables, loans, tax obligations and other liabilities at the valuation date.
5. Active signed contracts, renewal and termination terms, and outstanding delivery obligations.
6. Executed YaBatho terms, payment history, current ownership evidence and venture financial information.
7. Evidence identifying reusable software or other intellectual property retained by LPTB after client handovers.

A valuation can then consider earnings or cash flow, relevant transaction comparisons and net assets without double-counting the same economic benefit. These are standard valuation approaches described by the [Business Development Bank of Canada](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/start-buy-business/buy-business/how-value-company).

## Suitable wording for the company profile

“LPTB Solutions has experience delivering business websites, digital support services and connected software platforms. Its YaBatho engagement carries a documented project consideration of R250,000, supported by commercial and handover records. The company also provides recurring digital and technical support to The Chairman Group.”

This wording presents the evidence accurately without implying a valuation or a paid revenue total that the available records do not establish.
